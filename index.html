Questions That Shape Recommendations

A discussion with a mortgage broker should yield clear reasons for their recommendations, which borrowers should be able to articulate in simple terms. The published FAQ indicates that Australian mortgage brokers are legally obligated to act in the borrower's best interests and must explain the rationale behind any loan recommendation. It is important to ask what options were compared, which were ruled out, and what specific factors could alter the recommendation. For refinancing scenarios, inquire about the comparison of total costs, loan features, discharge fees, setup costs, and the justification for any debt consolidation. When considering an investment loan, ask how the proposed loan structure aligns with your investment portfolio, whether interest-only repayments are being considered, and how the lender assesses the application. For self-employed borrowers, clarify whether full documentation, alternative documentation, or low documentation pathways align with the available evidence. Construction loan applicants should ask about the process for progress payments, valuation timelines, required builder documentation, and how the lender handles house and land packages. Bridging loan applicants should have a plan in place for scenarios where the existing property sells later than anticipated.

Broker Comparison vs. Direct Application

Applying directly to a bank can be a straightforward process if the borrower has a clear preference for that bank's products and appears to meet its lending criteria. However, this approach offers a more limited selection of loan options. As stated in the published FAQ, a bank will only present its own range of loans, whereas a mortgage broker can compare products from multiple lenders and explain their suitability for your specific situation. Information from ASIC Moneysmart regarding home loans serves as valuable background, assisting borrowers in understanding loan costs, features, and repayment obligations before committing to a loan. A conversation with a broker should leave the borrower with a clear understanding of the repayment type, associated fees, loan conditions, the primary purpose of the loan, and the specific reasons why a particular option was favoured. This distinction becomes particularly important for applications involving variable income, construction finance, complex investment structures, debt consolidation, or the sale of an existing property. In such cases, the lender's policy considerations can be as significant as the advertised interest rate.

  1. Define Your Loan Goal. Clearly state whether your objective is to buy your first home, refinance an existing loan, purchase an investment property, finance a construction project, manage self-employed income, or arrange bridging finance.
  2. Outline Your Property Plan. Describe the type of property involved: an established home, an off-the-plan purchase, a new build, a house and land package, or an existing property you intend to refinance.
  3. Summarise Your Income. Provide details of your income sources, whether it's PAYG employment, contracting work, ABN holder income, business profits, or a combination of household incomes.
  4. Identify Your Main Constraint. Note any significant limitations, such as deposit size, availability of income evidence, existing debts, the need to sell a current property, a specific build contract, or uncertainty about repayment types (fixed, variable, interest-only, P&I).
  5. Prepare for the Enquiry. Write down your loan purpose, property plan, preferred timing, and main constraint. Ask about how the broker is compensated, if any fees apply, and when this will be disclosed in writing. Inquire about what could strengthen your application before it's submitted to a lender. Remember, the initial enquiry is for gathering information and does not constitute a commitment.

Common questions

Is it better to use a mortgage broker or go directly to a bank for a home loan? A mortgage broker can compare loans from multiple lenders, helping you find a product that suits your situation, whereas a bank will only offer its own loans. In Australia, brokers are required to act in your best interests and explain why a recommended loan is suitable. Many Illawarra borrowers use brokers to save time and improve their chances of approval. The choice often depends on whether you prefer broader choice and guidance or dealing directly with a single lender.

Do mortgage brokers in Wollongong charge fees or are they paid by the bank? Most Australian mortgage brokers are paid a commission by the lender and do not charge a direct fee to standard home loan customers. However, some brokers may charge a fee for complex lending or specialised advice, and they should disclose this clearly upfront. It is reasonable to ask a Wollongong broker exactly how they are paid and whether any fees apply to your specific situation. This helps you understand potential conflicts of interest and the overall costs involved.
